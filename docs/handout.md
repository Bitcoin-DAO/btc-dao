# What is a DAO?

November 2026 · [Italiano](handout.it.md) · One-page handout, summarised from [What is a Bitcoin DAO?](what-is-a-bitcoin-dao.md). When that page changes, check this one.

**Everything needed to run a small cooperative, without the paperwork.**

A DAO is a group of people who share a purpose and a shared pot of money, and run both together through a simple app instead of a registered company.

## What DAO stands for

- **Decentralised:** no head office and no single person in charge of the money. Spending needs several keyholders to agree.
- **Autonomous:** the group runs by its own rules, which the members set and change by vote. No registry, bank or notary has to approve them.
- **Organisation:** a group of people working towards something together, like a club, an association or a co-op.

The money is held in bitcoin, in a shared wallet that only moves when enough of the group's chosen keyholders sign.

## How it works

**Members** discuss, propose and vote. The **council**, a few members chosen by the group, hold the keys to the shared wallet: for example, any 3 of 5 must sign before money moves.

1. A member posts an idea and the group discusses it in the app.
2. The idea becomes a proposal: a payment, a new member, a rule change.
3. Members vote over a few days; each DAO sets its own voting period.
4. If it passes, the council signs and the payment goes out in the next payout round.
5. Everything stays on a public record: who proposed, who voted what, and where the money went.

## A co-op and a DAO compared

| | Small co-op (Italy) | DAO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| To start | Notary, statute, two registers; weeks | A founding meeting; a day |
| Changing the rules | Back to the notary | Members vote |
| Money and accounts | Bank account, accountant, annual statement | Shared bitcoin wallet, every payment public as it happens |
| Setting up | About €2,500 | About €60–90 for one shared signing device |
| Each year | About €2,800–4,500 | About €0–15, plus network fees |
| Legal status | Legal entity, limited liability | Not a legal entity |

Co-op figures are published estimates and vary; DAO figures are approximate. Sources are in the full page.

## Good to know

- **Not a legal entity.** A DAO cannot sign contracts or own property in its own name. Anyone who acts for the group does so in their own name, so the DAO deals with people and suppliers who accept bitcoin.
- **Public by design.** Votes and payments are visible to anyone. Members can use a nickname instead of their legal name.
- **Best for small groups** with a shared purpose and a shared pot: a community project, a club, a group of makers or neighbours. A group that needs employees, bank loans or public grants still needs a registered form.
